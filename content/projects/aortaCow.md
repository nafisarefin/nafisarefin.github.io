This project traces how emboli released during cardiopulmonary bypass (CPB) travel from the thoracic aorta into the cerebral circulation, all the way to the Circle of Willis (CoW), the arterial ring that supplies the brain. Because occlusion of these vessels is a direct pathway to peri-operative stroke, the study connects surgical inflow conditions to downstream cerebral occlusion risk in a single, anatomically continuous model.

An integrated experimental and computational framework was developed spanning the aorta and the Circle of Willis. Hemodynamics and embolic transport were simulated using Computational Fluid Dynamics (CFD) with Lagrangian Particle Tracking (LPT) in OpenFOAM, and the model was validated against in-vitro measurements in a patient-specific mock circulatory loop under representative CPB flow conditions.

The work demonstrates proficiency in patient-specific geometry reconstruction, mesh convergence analysis, CFD/LPT configuration, and cerebral hemodynamic post-processing. By linking aortic inflow to particle distribution across the cerebral branches, the model identifies conditions that elevate cerebral occlusion risk and provides a validated basis for evaluating strategies to reduce stroke during cardiac surgery.
