This research explores how emboli particles that can cause strokes move through the aorta during cardiopulmonary bypass (CPB), a common surgical technique used in open-heart procedures. By simulating a patient-specific aortic anatomy, this study identifies how variations in blood flow can alter embolic risk in the brain. The goal is to support safer surgical planning through predictive modeling of embolus trajectories.

A combined experimental and computational model was developed, using a 3D-printed patient-specific aorta model and mock circulatory loop for in-vitro validation. Computational fluid dynamics (CFD) simulations were conducted in OpenFOAM with Lagrangian Particle Tracking (LPT) to model embolus movement under different CPB flow conditions.

This project integrates skills in CAD modeling, mesh generation, CFD solver configuration, and LPT-based embolus transport analysis. The results show that embolic risk is strongly influenced by particle size, blood viscosity, and flow rate, with the Stokes number emerging as a reliable metric for trajectory prediction. This framework offers valuable understanding for optimizing CPB surgical protocols and reducing embolic complications during cardiac surgery.
